Lateral & axial pile analysis · Windows

Piles and drilled shafts, analysed end to end.

PileAxis analyses a single drilled shaft, precast pretensioned pile or steel H-pile under combined axial and lateral loads in layered soil. It solves the p-y response coupled with the axial response for every load combination, checks the section to ACI 318-25 or AISC 360-22, and audits the soil and the equilibrium independently, with the reference behind every result.

What it does, free

Every check, from one converged solution.

Every load combination is solved once and then examined from every side: the section's strength, the soil's capacity, the deflection and settlement, and an equilibrium audit that does not trust the solver.

Soil

Layered soil that follows the literature.

Build the soil column layer by layer from eight built-in p-y models, each with its own parameters, under a water table that sets the effective stress for you.

  • Soft clay (Matlock), stiff clay below and above the water table, sand (the full Reese curve), API sand, weak rock and strong rock, and a linear soil
  • The Georgiadis correction at layer interfaces, and AASHTO group p-multipliers per pile
  • Buoyant unit weights and effective stress below the water table, worked for you
  • Copy a soil column to a spreadsheet, edit it there and paste it back
  • Matlock (1970)
  • Reese et al. (1974, 1975)
  • Georgiadis (1983)

Section strength

ACI 318-25 on a Mander-confined fiber section.

A drilled shaft is checked at every node of the pile, for every strength combination: a full axial-moment interaction diagram and the shear check with the axial load's contribution.

  • The stiffness of each node taken from the section's moment-curvature response, so a cracked pile is analysed as a cracked pile
  • Reinforcement by bar count and size or by a target steel ratio
  • Confined and ordinary zones of transverse reinforcement, each with its own spacing
  • The demand at every node plotted against the interaction diagram
  • ACI 318-25 §22.4
  • §22.5
  • §13.3.2.1
  • Mander et al. (1988)

Checks

A governing case for each kind of check, not one number for all.

The section is judged on the strength combinations, the soil's capacity on every combination against a resistance-factored capacity, and deflection and settlement on the service combinations, each with its own governing case.

  • Lateral p-y springs, shaft skin friction and tip bearing each screened against their ultimate capacity
  • Every converged solution re-integrated for force and moment balance, the P-delta moment included
  • Status lights for the section, the soil, service and the equilibrium audit
  • A numbered governing report and CSV export for every run

Lateral and axial, coupled

A finite-difference beam on nonlinear p-y springs, coupled with t-z and Q-z springs, so the axial load's effect on bending is carried through.

An audit that does not trust the solver

Each solution is checked for equilibrium by a separate calculation, so a sign or discretization error cannot hide behind a converged residual.

Service and LRFD, routed for you

Tag each combination once; strength checks read the factored ones and deflection checks the service ones, all solved in parallel.

Spreadsheet in, spreadsheet out

Soil layers and load cases copy to a spreadsheet and paste back; every chart copies as a picture or as its data.

The full manual, built in

Methods, equations, assumptions, limits and the validation, written so you can verify the engineering yourself, open beside your work.

Pro · New

Driven piles: precast pretensioned concrete and steel H-piles.

Analyse a driven pile with the same coupled lateral and axial solution as a drilled shaft. Its axial capacity comes from the FHWA driven-pile static methods, and its section is checked to the code that governs it.

  • PCI square and octagonal piles, solid or voided, to ACI 318-25's prestressed provisions, with PCI's spiral check
  • AISC HP shapes or custom plates, strong or weak axis, to AISC 360-22: compression, flexure, shear and their interaction
  • Axial capacity by FHWA GEC-12: Nordlund in sand, the alpha method in clay, or the beta method in either
  • Driving-stress limits reported for information, and the FHWA driven-pile resistance factors in one click
Pro

Push each load case to failure.

The pushover traces each load case's full lateral capacity curve, pushing the head by displacement so the curve runs past its peak, with the axial load held at the case's own value.

  • The ultimate lateral capacity and the reserve beyond your design load
  • First yield and plastic hinges, marked on the curve with their depths
  • Curvature ductility, and whether the section or the soil gives way first

Pro · AI Assistant

An assistant grounded in your project, not in guesses.

The AI Assistant is a chat window beside the program. It reads your pile, soil, section and loads, your results and your pushover, and answers from them: why a case governs, what a ratio means, how a method works, and where to look next.

  • Every number it states is read from your project or its results; one it cannot trace is marked unverified in the chat
  • Methods and how-to questions are answered from the same User's Manual you can open yourself
  • It searches the published papers behind the methods and cites the passage, a click from the source
  • It finds your other cloud projects by distance to each pile, by pile, soil or load properties, or by a description of the work

The assistant reads your project and cannot change it. Its answers carry the program's engineering disclaimer: review by a licensed engineer is required. Your question and the project data it needs are sent to Microsoft's Azure AI service to answer it; we count your usage against a monthly allowance and do not keep the conversation. New · Your own AI assistant

Hand your AI assistant the geotechnical report. It builds the pile model.

Connect an AI assistant that accepts custom MCP connectors, Claude among them, and give it the geotechnical report. It reads the boring logs and creates a complete pile analysis in your cloud project: the whole soil profile, the water table, the pile, its section and its load cases, checked by PileAxis's own rules.

  • Every soil layer, top down, with the p-y model its stratum calls for and that model's own inputs
  • Checked with the Soil, Pile, Section and Loads tabs' own checks before anything is saved
  • A value the report does not give is asked of you, never guessed or taken from an example
  • Saved unrun, in your name: open it, check each layer against the report, and run it

How to connect your assistant is explained in the program's user's manual (Help > User's Manual). It works as you, with your own account. Creating a plain pile analysis needs only an account; a driven pile or a tabular p-y curve needs Pro, as at the desk. How the assistant read the boring logs is its reading: check it as you would a colleague's.

New · What-if on your computer

Ask it for a what-if. Your computer runs it.

With PileAxis open and you signed in, your assistant can study the pile analysis you have open: change what you ask, run the versions on your own computer with PileAxis's own analysis, compare them, and propose the one that works. You click Apply, or Decline.

  • Off until you allow it on each computer; every request is listed where you can watch it and cancel it
  • Up to eight changed versions at a time, each checked first and run on its own copy of your project
  • A proposal shows every change and its results beside yours, and one Ctrl+Z takes an applied one back
  • In your cloud, it also searches every project you can open, of both programs

Running what-ifs and searching by project need only an account; searching by a pile analysis's inputs needs Pro. Your open project never changes unless you click Apply.
